About the lab

Rigorous economics with direct policy application

The lab studies how governments can use taxes, transfers, debt, and public spending to improve economic outcomes across households, generations, and the broader economy.

The Macro Public Finance Lab @ ANU develops structural models that connect micro-level heterogeneity to macroeconomic dynamics. Our research is designed to clarify the distributional, behavioural, and aggregate consequences of fiscal policy.

We work at the intersection of academic research and policy engagement. That includes the use of administrative data, lifecycle modelling, and institutionally grounded policy counterfactuals relevant to Australia and comparable economies.

1
Micro-level evidence
Administrative records and household data are used to discipline model behaviour and policy simulations.
2
Structural modelling
Lifecycle and overlapping-generations models quantify long-run responses to fiscal reforms.
3
Policy evaluation
Distributional, welfare, and macro effects are studied jointly rather than in isolation.

Training

Methods, computation, and policy application

The lab's training program equips graduate students, early-career researchers, and policy economists with practical tools for frontier research in macro public finance.

Training combines quantitative macroeconomic theory with computational implementation, calibration, and policy simulation. The goal is to help participants move from model intuition to applied research practice.

Workshops and seminars are built around the same methods used in the lab's own research on tax policy, transfers, pensions, and inequality.
